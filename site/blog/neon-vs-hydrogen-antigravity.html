The release of Gemini 4 Neon provides a new and better option for vibe-coders using Google's Antigravity. Analysis shows that Neon gives more intelligence for less quota than all of the Gemini 4 predecessors. For anything above Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Low reasoning, Neon will get more done, with a higher Artificial Analysis Intelligence score.

I wanted to see how much you could actually get done with different models in Antigravity without running into the weekly limit. To do this I measured how quickly each model uses the weekly limit and compared that to the results on Artificial Analysis. The result is this graph, showing the number of tasks in the Artificial Analysis suite each model can do in a month's worth of Google AI Pro subscription.

The "Pareto line" shows where you get the most tasks done at the highest intelligence level. If you want above GPT-6 Luna intelligence, Neon is always the best choice. You get 352 tasks/month @ Low, 154 tasks/month @ Medium, 78 @ High, and 45 @ xHigh reasoning levels. Time to switch to Neon, without needing to worry about usage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I use Gemini 4 Neon or Hydrogen in Google Antigravity?

Use Gemini 4 Neon by default. Neon Low (44.1 intelligence, ~352 tasks/mo) beats Hydrogen Medium (41.2 intelligence, ~263 tasks/mo), and Neon Medium (54.9 intelligence, ~154 tasks/mo) beats Hydrogen xHigh (53.9 intelligence, ~66 tasks/mo) by 2.3×.

When should I still pick Gemini 4 Hydrogen?

Only on Hydrogen Low (~718 tasks/mo, 26.9 intelligence) when you're doing simple, repetitive tasks and want the maximum possible task count from your weekly allowance.

What about Claude models in Antigravity?

Google AI Pro includes a separate weekly third-party quota pool for Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 (roughly $20/mo in equivalent API value — about 8 tasks/mo on Opus 5.5 Medium or 25 tasks/mo on Sonnet 5.5 Medium). Treat it as a useful bonus pool alongside Neon rather than your main daily driver.

Are these numbers theoretical or measured?

They are anchored on real multi-turn Antigravity sessions running autonomous coding tasks in Docker, measuring exact weekly quota burn against API token cost, then scaled onto Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5 tasks. All code and raw CSVs are open-source on GitHub.
